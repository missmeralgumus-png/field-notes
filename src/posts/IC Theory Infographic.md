# Interactional Competence

Not what one person **knows** — what two people **build together**, turn by turn.

01 · The core idea

## Competence lives between people, not inside one

Successful talk reduces each speaker's uncertainty about the other's intentions — a shared sphere of intersubjectivity.

Young (2011): IC is the *pragmatic relationship between participants' resources and contexts*, used mutually and reciprocally. Kramsch (1986) first called it a "sphere of inter-subjectivity."

04 · What participants draw on

## Three kinds of resource

### Identity

The participation framework — who may speak, who is addressed, the roles the talk makes relevant.

### Linguistic

Register and the practice's own ways of meaning — the words and structures that belong to this encounter.

### Interactional

The machinery of talk — speech acts, turn-taking, repair, and the signalling of boundaries.

05 · The machinery (from Conversation Analysis)

## Four organisations do the interactional work

### Turn-taking

Taking, holding, and yielding the floor; timing an entry; backchannelling to keep the other going.

…right, mhm — may I jump in?

### Sequence organisation

Building actions across turns and projecting what's coming — openings, prefaces, topic shifts.

by the way… that reminds me…

### Repair

Handling trouble in speaking, hearing, or understanding — the goal is smooth self-repair and try-marking.

sorry, do you mean… the Tuesday invoice?

### Preference organisation

Softening dispreferred actions — delays, hedges, accounts, and "yes-but" instead of a bald "No."

yeah, no, I see your point — but…

06 · How it develops

## Not a straight line

The same learner can look *less* competent mid-way — regression is often reorganisation (Cekaite, 2007).

Recalibrated, not transferred

Adults rework their **L1** interactional methods for new resources and norms — they don't build from zero.

New purpose, not new form

What develops is often the **job a form does**: a learner who had *but* to grab turns later uses it to mitigate — diversifying methods for **recipient design**.

07 · Why it matters

## Pragmatic slips are read as character, not language

**A pragmatic slip**a mistimed interruption, an unmitigated "no"

→

**Not heard as "still learning"**listeners don't file it as a language error

→

**Attributed to the person**rude · evasive · cold — Thomas's (1983) pragmatic failure

This is why IC is taught directly: it is where a learner's humanity is read, and where misunderstanding does the most damage.
